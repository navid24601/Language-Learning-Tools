# Common grammar contrasts

Use when the learner asks about one of these contrasts. Examples illustrate a meaning in context; they are not exhaustive rules.

- **Past simple / present perfect:** “I visited Rome in 2022” locates an event at a finished time. “I have visited Rome twice” describes experience up to now; no finished time is specified. “I have lived here for five years” normally means I still live here.
- **Present simple / progressive:** “She works at a café” describes a usual situation. “She is working at the café this week” describes a situation in progress or temporary in that context. Some verbs are less commonly used in progressive forms for particular senses, not categorically impossible.
- **Articles:** “I saw a dog. The dog was sleeping” introduces one dog, then refers back to it. Article choice also depends on countability, specificity and context; do not reduce it to first versus second mention in every case.
- **Modals:** “You must leave now” expresses a strong obligation in this context; “You might leave early” expresses possibility. *Must* can also express a deduction (“She must be tired”), so explain the intended sense.

Ask for context when more than one form is plausible. Contrast form and meaning, then provide a brief opportunity to apply the distinction only if wanted.
