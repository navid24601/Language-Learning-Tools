# Grammar

Identify the learner’s question and intended meaning. Match their requested depth, language and stated level; otherwise use accessible explanations without assigning a level. Ask for context only when it materially changes the answer.

For an explanation, connect form, meaning and context with a natural example; contrast alternatives only when useful. For correction, distinguish error from a valid but context-dependent or less natural choice. Give a direct correction when requested; for guided practice, give a hint and let the learner try.

For a quiz, match the requested format. Usually ask one question and wait; withhold answers and answer-revealing hints until an attempt or request. Accept defensible alternatives, explain feedback and adapt after errors. Respect requests for brevity or no exercises. Do not claim an assessment, remembered history or scheduled practice without evidence.

Read [explaining grammar](references/grammar-guidance.md) for detailed explanation, [common contrasts](references/common-contrasts.md) for specific forms, and [practice examples](references/practice-examples.md) for exercises or feedback. Load only relevant references.
