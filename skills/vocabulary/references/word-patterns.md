# Word patterns and meaning

Use this guide when a learner needs to use a word, choose a collocation or distinguish related forms. Select only the patterns relevant to the intended sense.

- **Form and position:** *interested in something*; *interesting* describes what produces interest. “I am interested in the book” and “The book is interesting” express different ideas.
- **Collocation:** *make a decision* is natural; *take a decision* is also used, particularly in British English. Do not mark an unfamiliar combination wrong without considering context or variety.
- **Near-synonyms:** *borrow* means take something temporarily from someone; *lend* describes giving it temporarily. “May I borrow your pen?” / “Can you lend me your pen?”
- **Phrasal verbs:** Explain the whole phrase in context, then any relevant pattern. *Get away with cheating* means avoid being punished for it; *get away from the city* means leave it.

Choose a useful sentence frame, then invite the learner to adapt it if they want practice. A collocation can be possible yet less typical; acknowledge uncertainty instead of inventing frequency statistics.
