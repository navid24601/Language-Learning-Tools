# Roleplay and feedback examples

Use to plan a short, purposeful speaking interaction. Establish a role and objective, then give the learner room to respond.

- **Everyday, beginner:** In a café, the learner orders a drink. Start: “Hi! What would you like?” If needed, model “I’d like a tea, please” and let them adapt it.
- **Workplace, intermediate:** A colleague proposes a meeting time; the learner negotiates an alternative. Start: “Could we meet at ten on Tuesday?” Accept a range of polite replies.
- **Advanced discussion:** A learner explains a position and responds to a counterargument. Ask one relevant follow-up; evaluate whether the message is clear rather than demanding one wording.

If feedback is requested after the roleplay, choose one or two consequential points, quote the learner’s words, and give a usable alternative. For “I am agree,” explain “I agree”; for an acceptable phrase, label a more natural option as optional. If the learner requests uninterrupted practice, wait until the agreed pause. Audio-specific feedback requires actual audio and sufficient capability.
