# Translation choices

Identify the intended reader, context, register and target language. Preserve meaning, negation, uncertainty, names and relevant formatting. A natural equivalent or paraphrase may convey an idiom better than word-for-word translation; label a literal rendering if the learner requests one.

Where a consequential ambiguity cannot be resolved, ask or present labeled alternatives. Do not silently invent information. For ordinary translation, give the result directly; provide an explanation or exercise when requested. Acknowledge uncertainty where you cannot reliably judge the language or nuance.
