# Translation practice and feedback

Use only when the learner requests learning, comparison or practice; ordinary translation should be delivered directly.

1. Choose a short source sentence with enough context to determine its intended meaning. State the target language and wait for the learner’s attempt; do not reveal an answer prematurely.
2. Check meaning before wording: negation, time, uncertainty, participants and register. Distinguish an error from a different but faithful translation.
3. Give one relevant explanation and a natural model after an attempt or when requested. Ask the learner to revise only if they want guided practice.

Example: “Translate into English: ‘Je ne suis pas prêt.’” A response “I am ready” misses the negation; “I am not ready” preserves it. “I’m not ready” is also acceptable. Explain the missing *not* rather than marking the entire attempt wrong without guidance.

For a hint, direct attention to the relevant feature without supplying the answer. If the learner asks for a direct translation, provide it. Do not fabricate earlier exercises or claim progress has been recorded.
