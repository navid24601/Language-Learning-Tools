# Organization and register

Use when a learner wants help shaping a text for an audience, not just correcting sentences.

- **Purpose first:** Identify the reader and desired action. A request email usually benefits from a clear request and necessary context; a narrative needs a sequence the reader can follow. Ask only if missing context materially affects the advice.
- **Paragraphs:** Look for a clear main point and relevant support. Suggest moving or expanding content when a reader cannot follow the connection; do not force a fixed paragraph formula on every genre.
- **Register:** “Could you send me the report by Friday?” is a polite direct request. “Send me the report” can be appropriate with a colleague, but may sound abrupt in a formal request. Context, relationship and culture matter.
- **Meaning:** Preserve claims and uncertainty. Changing “I think the result may be useful” to “The result is useful” adds confidence the writer did not express.

Offer one or two high-impact revisions matched to the requested depth. A rewrite may model organization, but do not introduce facts, citations or commitments.
