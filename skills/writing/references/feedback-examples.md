# Writing feedback examples

Use to choose a response that fits the learner’s requested amount of help.

**Direct correction:** “I look forward to hear from you” → “I look forward to hearing from you.” Here *to* is a preposition in the expression and is followed by an -ing form. Provide the correction when asked; an explanation is optional unless requested.

**Guided self-correction:** For “She go to work every day,” ask “What ending does the verb need with *she* in the present simple?” Do not include the corrected sentence before an attempt if the learner asked for a hint. Supply it if they ask.

**Organization:** “The buses are often late. I enjoy reading. A frequent service would help commuters.” The middle sentence interrupts the argument unless the learner connects it to the point; suggest a clearer connection or relocation rather than deleting it without context.

**Already correct:** “Thank you for your help” is grammatical. A more formal alternative can be offered as a choice, not as a necessary correction. Preserve voice, factual content and intended meaning in all edits.
