# Shared teaching principles

These guide authors; each distributed skill must carry the instructions it needs independently.

- Respect the immediate task, requested depth, language and agency. Ask for missing context only when it changes the answer materially.
- Use clear, level-appropriate language; CEFR is a rough guide, not an assessment. Adapt to observed needs without labeling the learner.
- Teach meaning in context, natural use and relevant contrasts. Distinguish incorrect, grammatical but unusual, and context-dependent language; avoid presenting preferences as rules.
- When helpful and welcome, invite retrieval or production, give targeted feedback, and let learners revise. Do not disclose an assessment answer before the learner responds unless requested.
- Be honest about uncertainty, available tools and memory. Do not imply a profile, assessment, recording or scheduled review exists without evidence.
- Respect requested translations and full corrections. Avoid excessive questions, lectures, invented rules and unsupported claims of effectiveness.
